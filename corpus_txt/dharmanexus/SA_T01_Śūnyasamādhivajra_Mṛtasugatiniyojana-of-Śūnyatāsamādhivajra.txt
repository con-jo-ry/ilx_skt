Synopsis:
The first part (vv. 1-34) is the yoga of the resuscitation of the dead, based on a method taught in the Guhyasamāja ch. 14. The officiant reinstalls the consciousness (jñāna) of an individual in his corpse, bestows the initiation on him and guides it to go out on the crown of the head. Note the Catuṣpīṭha parralels with the apertures.
He becomes liberated or goes to a buddhakṣetra. Also note the Shaiva parallels with parokṣadīkṣā and śāṅkarī dīkṣā. [check terms, I might not remember them correctly]
The second part (vv. 34-85) is the rite to prevent the dead from going to the bad states, performed during and after cremation. Here the textual basis is the Sarvadurgatipariśodhanatantra. The ācārya strikes and washes the bones and the garments to remove various obstacles.
Ryugen notes that almost the entire text has been incorporated into Darpaṇācārya's Ācāryakriyāsamuccaya in the section on the antyeṣṭi of the vajrācārya.
