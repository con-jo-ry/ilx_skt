Abhayākaragupta's Varṣāpaṇavidhi
After meditating on emptiness, on a multi-coloured lotus and a sundisk, a Hūṃ turns into Vajragaruḍa - fierce, 16 year old, winged, black body, four faced ('first' dark; left smoky; right white; upper is a garuḍa-head, red), eight armed (R1: vajra, L1: pāśatarjanī, RL2: hide, R3: skull, L3: skull-staff, R4: goad, L4:
ḍamaru), piled hair topped with a cross-vajra and a moon crescent, wears 6 bone ornaments, a tiger hide, stands in ālīḍha pose on white Varuṇa and the tail of a nāga [?].
His retinue - E white Ananta, N dark Vāsuki, W red Takṣaka, S green Padma, NE white Mahāpadma, NW smoky Śaṅkhapāla, SE grey Karkoṭa, SW yellow Anantatapta. [check colours and intermediate directions]. All are three-hooded, hands folded, awaiting the lord's command, frightened.
He should imagine the mantra hūṃ naṃ hūṃ in the hearts of the 9 attendants, while facing east in front of a paṭa of the lord. Then he should do the prior service of the given mantra (one lakh).
After perfecting the mantra he should meditate fiercely, composedly, keeping silent, celibate, then sacrifice milk, etc. into fire at each junction, whilst reciting 10,000 times the given mantras of the attendants. He should imagine [?] the Garuḍa ... [?] them. Another mantra to be recited. Seven days later it will rain.
Another procedure, without smoke [i.e. homa-fire?] is given to stop excessive rain. Mantras given. At the end [of both procedures?] a pūjā is performed and the śatākṣara recited [to make up for shortcomings].
NB: lineage given: Vanaratna, Sarvamitra, Ravicandra, and Rūparāja, the scribe.
