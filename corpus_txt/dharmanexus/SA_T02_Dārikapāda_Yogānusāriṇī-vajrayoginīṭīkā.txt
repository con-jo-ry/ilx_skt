A work on Vajrayoginī, the title and the author are lost. The commencing parts bear some similarity with Tōh. 1565 (*Yogānusāriṇī nāma vajrayoginīṭīkā of *Dārikapāda).
In the introduction, which is not devoid of some poetic merit, the author mentions `the emperor Indrabhūti' and his Ḍākinīrahasya as well as his unnamed master's work on and called Svādhiṣṭhāna. To this, he says, he wrote a commentary called Svādhiṣṭhānavyākhyā, which probably still survives in China and contains the name of the author, *Kuṭali.
The quote from his master's work matches a line in Tōh. 2275, where it is attributed to Saraha. After the lengthy introduction he teaches sādhana, mantroddharaṇa, japa, bali, tilaka, udvartana, añjana, and finally caryā. It is in this section that the testimony breaks off, probably not much is lacking.
The work must have been popular, since there are three fragments to be found in Nepal, one of them (here A) almost complete. Ms. B also contains a one-folio fragment of an essay on Mahāmudrā, it is uncertain whether it was part of this work.
