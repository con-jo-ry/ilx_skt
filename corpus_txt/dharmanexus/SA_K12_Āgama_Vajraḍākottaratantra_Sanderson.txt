Partial transcription and notes by AGJSS, 17–18 February, 2008:
Translated  by Shā kya brtson 'grus in the presence of the  Indian
Mahāpaṇḍita *Vareśvara (Mchog gi dbang phyug) in a hermitage on the
slopes of the Lha ri mo in Grom pa.  Shā kya brtson 'grus is a scholar of the
11th century, if we may rely on the information of other translation colophons,
according to which he translated the Abhiṣekanirukti (Toh. 2477) and a
*Mahāmāyāsādhana (Sgyu 'phrul chen mo zhes bya ba'i sgrub pa'i thabs, Toh.
1626) with Avadhūtipā (=Advayavajra), who lived 1007/1010–1085/88, and the
*Mahāyānaviṃśati (Theg pa chen po nyi shu pa, Toh. 2248) with
Devākaracandra (=Śūnyatāsamādhi), a pupil of Advayavajra,  and one of the
teachers of Rong zom chos kyi bzang po, who lived 1012–1088. He also
translated a *Pañcakramapañjikå (Rim pa lnga'i dka' 'grel: Toh. no. 1838)
and a *Pradīpoddyotanapañjikā (Sgron ma gsal bar byed pa'i dka' ba btus pa'i
'grel pa, Toh. no. 1792) with *Jinavara (Rgyal ba mchog), which contradicts the
assertion in 'Tibskrit' that the latter was active in the second half of the
12th century.
The translation is not in the Narthang (Eimer: western group) and Tog Palace
Kanjurs (Eimer: western group, but with some contamination with the eastern,
probably that of Derge), but is found in that of Derge ( Urga)
(Eimer: eastern group). It is also found in that of Lhasa. According to Eimer,
that is based mainly on the Narthang Kanjur but also draws readings from that
of Derge. If have yet to check whether it is found in the Peking group (Eimer:
eastern group), and the London manuscript Kanjur (Eimer: western group). It is
improbable that it is in the last, since that and the Derge are closely related
and derive from a common source.
Seeing the title Vajraḍākottara in the Kanjur one expects to find a work
quite distinct from the Vajraḍāka itself. But it has turned out to consist
almost entirely of extracts from the Vajraḍāka itself:
Paṭala 1 = Vajraḍāka Paṭala 1;
Paṭala 2 = Vajraḍāka Paṭala 2;
Paṭala 3 = the first part of Vajraḍāka Paṭala 3, deviating at the end;
Paṭala 12 = Vajraḍāka Paṭala 12 (Vajraḍākasādhanam).
Paṭala 14 (the last) = the first part of Vajraḍāka Paṭala 19 (Mātṛmaṇḍalam).
Paṭalas 4–11 and 13 are extremely short (pp. 263,7–266,2). I have not
recognized their provenance. I do not recall them in the Vajraḍāka, but have
not checked thoroughly.
The 13th Paṭala has some matter seen in Vajraḍāka Paṭala 18.
This situation is reflected in the colophons. Those of all but the third
Paṭala and the last (14th) refer to the text simply as the
Vajraḍākamahātantra. That of Paṭala 3 says that it is the rdo rje mkha'
'gro ma'i rgyud chen po (*Vajraḍākinīmahātantra). But this is probably the
result of a error in which a copyist wrote mkha' 'gro ma instead of the less
common mkha' 'gro. The colophon of the 14th Paṭala calls the text the rdo rje
mkha' 'gro'i snying po. The colophon of the whole work, which immediately
follows, calls it the rdo rje mkha' 'gro'i rgyud phyi ma snying po bsdus pa.
The term snying po could render various Sanskrit words: hṛdaya, garbha,
maṇḍa, or sāra. But the combination snying po bsdus probably renders
sārasaṃgraha. The title in the colophon of the 14th, then, was probably
Vajraḍākasāra and that in the final colophon
Vajraḍākatantrottarasārasaṃgraha.  Perhaps the sense intended was `A
collection the core teachings [from the Vajraḍāka], as a supplement to the
Vajraḍāka itself'.

This is the only occurrence of the name *Vajraḍākottara in the text, all the
preceding Paṭalas saying that they belong to the Vajraḍāka, with the
exception of one that claims to be from the Vajraḍākinītantra (probably a
scribal error). One may therefore question the attribution of this title to the
whole.
